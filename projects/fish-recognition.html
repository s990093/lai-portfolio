Fish Recognition System:
High-Efficiency Detection

An optimized fish recognition system overcoming data scarcity and small object challenges. Leveraging RT-DETR and a two-stage ConvNeXt-Tiny post-processing strategy to achieve +18.31% Precision.

01. Methodology: RT-DETR

Fish detection presents unique challenges: targets are often small (width/height < 0.4) and densely distributed. Traditional detectors like YOLO often struggle with low IoU recall in these scenarios.

Figure 1: Bounding Box Size Distribution

Analysis showing the prevalence of small objects in the dataset, necessitating a model capable of fine-grained detection.

Figure 2: RT-DETR Architecture

RT-DETR combines a CNN backbone with a hybrid encoder, using AIFI and CCFM for effective multi-scale feature extraction.

We selected RT-DETR (Real-Time DEtection TRansformer) for its end-to-end architecture, anchor-free design, and superior performance in dense environments.

02. Two-Stage Training Strategy

With only ~100 labeled images available, training a deep model from scratch was infeasible. We implemented a two-stage training approach:

Phase 1: Pre-training

Utilized 30,000+ images from Roboflow to initialize the model weights, enabling it to learn general fish features.

Phase 2: Fine-tuning

Fine-tuned on the 100 high-quality labeled images using extensive augmentation (MixUp, Mosaic, Gaussian Blur) and DDP on 2x NVIDIA A4000 GPUs.

Figure 3: Advanced Data Augmentation

Techniques like glare removal (HSV) and inpainting (DeepFillv2) were used to handle water reflections and occlusions.

03. Two-Stage Post-Processing

Despite strong detection performance, the model produced False Positives (FP) in high-glare areas. Analysis revealed these FPs typically had low IoU confidence.

Figure 4: TP vs FP Distribution

Most False Positives (orange) cluster in low IoU regions, suggesting they can be filtered by a secondary classifier.

Figure 5: Low IoU Samples

Examples of 'suspicious' detections that require further verification.

Solution: ConvNeXt-Tiny Classifier

We implemented a cascade system:

  1. Detection: RT-DETR proposes bounding boxes.
  2. Filtering: Low-confidence boxes are cropped.
  3. Verification: ConvNeXt-Tiny classifies crops as "Fish" or "Background".

Figure 6: ConvNeXt-Tiny Architecture

A lightweight, pure-ConvNet model inspired by Vision Transformers, providing high-speed secondary verification.

04. Experimental Results

RT-DETR vs. YOLO

RT-DETR significantly outperforms YOLO, achieving an AP of 0.908 (vs 0.772) and a peak F1 score of 0.862.

Figure 7: Precision-Recall Curve

RT-DETR (bold line) maintains higher precision across all recall levels compared to YOLO.

Figure 8: F1 Score vs Confidence

RT-DETR achieves a better F1 score balance at optimal confidence thresholds.
